Select “Compare models” to load results.

WF means walk-forward. This evaluates models across chronological validation windows, and the baseline is scored on exactly the same days. Lower MAE and RMSE are better. “Beats baseline?” uses the walk-forward results and a Diebold-Mariano test: “significant” means the improvement is unlikely to be luck (p < 0.05). Changing the horizon re-sorts the table instantly; all three horizons are computed together.

Is the range trustworthy?

A 90% range should contain the real price about 90% of the time. “Coverage” is how often it actually did on past walk-forward test days.

Horizon Method Coverage (target 90%) Average width Verdict Shown above?
Loading...

Two methods are compared. “Quantile Gradient Boosting” is a machine learning model trained to predict the lower and upper ends of the range. “Volatility-scaled history” is a simple method that scales past price moves by recent volatility. The method whose coverage is closest to 90% is the one shown in the cards above. “Too narrow” means the range is overconfident; “Too wide” means it is more cautious than needed.

Ranges describe typical uncertainty based on past behavior. Real prices can and sometimes do fall outside them, and this is not financial advice.
